# Summary

The podcast explores the economic implications of AI, particularly focusing on how it changes the nature of work, the structure of companies, and the skills needed to thrive. It highlights the shift from automation to verification, where AI handles routine tasks, and humans focus on nuanced judgment, intent, and "meaning-making." The discussion introduces the concept of the "AI sandwich," where a director oversees a swarm of AI agents, verified by human experts. The conversation touches on the "codifier's curse," where experts must continuously upgrade their skills to stay ahead of AI, and the potential for a "hollow economy" if investments aren't made in training future experts. Ultimately, the podcast posits an "augmented economy" where AI accelerates learning and humans focus on uniquely human skills, augmented by technology. The role of crypto in providing trust and verification in an AI-driven world is also examined.

# Takeaways

* AI agents can now perform long-running tasks, creating the sensation of working with a coworker, which provokes the imagination and forces people to grapple with the implications.
* The rise of AI is shifting the work of software engineers away from writing code and toward verification, ensuring code correctness, value, and alignment with business goals.
* Innovation increasingly involves recombining existing ideas, and AI agents can be extremely innovative by exploring the vast number of potential recombinations within humanity's accumulated knowledge.
* The "codifier's curse" describes the need for top verifiers to continuously move up the stack and add value as AI technology improves, staying one step ahead of automation.
* The augmented economy involves accelerating mastery through AI-driven personalized learning, enabling individuals to discover their true talents and passions.
* AI's ability to automate measurable tasks will drive the economy toward the cost of compute, making education and healthcare more accessible.
* Cryptography and blockchains are essential verification tools that provide provenance and make different categories of verification easier, helping to close the gap between automation and verification costs.
* Open source development plays a crucial role in identifying and addressing potential abuses and side effects of AI systems, enabling the development of countermeasures.
* As AI capabilities advance, every digital platform will need to grapple with distinguishing between human and agent contributions, necessitating a reimagining of the identity stack.
* A "verification-grade network effect" arises when proprietary data enables scaling verification from humans to machines, leading to better risk underwriting and safer, lower-cost products.

# Q & A

**Q: What is the main point of your economics paper on AGI, and what inspired you to write it?**

A: My paper, "Some Simple Economics of AGI," stems from a need to understand the rapid advancements in AI and their economic implications. It explores what the economy, labor, startups, and incumbents might look like if we take the current trend of automation to its limit. I felt it was crucial to address what we should focus on and how we can influence the trajectory of this technology meaningfully.

---

**Q: Eddy, how has the capability of AI agents changed recently, and what impact is this having on the workforce?**

A: The significant change occurred around December 2023, when AI agents became capable of performing long-running tasks with less guidance. Previously, you had to prompt the AI for each step, but now you can give it a task and let it work for a day or two. This feels like working with a coworker, provoking a lot of imagination and causing people to grapple with the reality of AI's capabilities and how to squeeze value from it in commercial settings.

---

**Q: How is the role of a software engineer evolving with the rise of AI, and what skills are becoming more critical?**

A: The role is shifting from writing code to ensuring the code works, is correct, and provides value to the customer. AI is taking over much of the code-writing process, but the nuanced, iterative process of correcting, straightening, gathering feedback, and integrating remains crucial. The balance of work is shifting towards verification, making sure the code serves business goals and handles prioritized tasks effectively.

---

**Q: What is the "codifier's curse," and how does it affect top verifiers in the age of AI?**

A: The codifier's curse describes the mechanic where top verifiers need to continuously move up the stack to stay ahead of the technology. As AI improves, verifiers must keep adding value at a higher level to remain one step ahead of the machine. This means constantly enhancing their skills and expertise to tackle increasingly complex challenges that AI cannot yet handle.

---

**Q: What is the "AI sandwich," and how does it describe the structure of a firm or startup in the age of AI?**

A: The "AI sandwich" refers to a firm structure comprising a director (or small team) at the top, a swarm of AI agents in the middle, and an army of top verifiers at the bottom. The director steers and ensures course correction, the agents produce work, and the verifiers ensure the intended outcomes are achieved.

---

**Q: What are the three categories of jobs that will be most affected by AI, and what are the implications for workers in each category?**

A: The three categories are verifiers, directors, and meaning-makers. Verifiers face the "codifier's curse" and must continuously upskill. Directors, such as entrepreneurs, drive intent and steer projects. Meaning-makers focus on social coordination and consensus-building. Jobs that are easy to automate and verify are likely to disappear, necessitating retraining and pushing people further up the knowledge frontier.

---

**Q: What is the role of "verification" in the context of AI, and why is it so important?**

A: Verification is applying embedded measurement from human experience to ensure AI outputs align with intent and context. It’s crucial because AI excels at replicating measured processes but struggles with unmeasured, nuanced aspects. Verification involves using human judgment, taste, and expertise to validate and correct AI-generated outputs, ensuring they meet the required standards and goals.

---

**Q: How does AI's ability to automate tasks impact the division between measurable and non-measurable tasks, and what are the implications for the future of work?**

A: AI is increasingly automating measurable tasks, pushing humans towards non-measurable areas that require consensus, judgment, and unique human experiences. This shift means that the value of human labor lies in areas where AI cannot easily replicate human insight and coordination, such as art, strategy, and complex problem-solving that requires a deep understanding of societal trends and values.

---

**Q: How does the concept of "consensus" play a role in the context of AI and human collaboration, and what are the potential challenges?**

A: Consensus is important for coordinating tasks, especially in areas where there isn't a clear right or wrong answer. While humans currently dominate consensus formation, AI could potentially coordinate faster by analyzing models and creating incentive schemes. The challenge lies in aligning machine and human consensus to ensure efficient and effective collaboration.

---

**Q: What is the "Trojan horse" externality, and what systemic risks does it pose to society as AI automation increases?**

A: The "Trojan horse" externality refers to the risk of accumulating systemic errors and technical debt as AI-generated outputs are deployed without full verification. Because it's impossible to review all AI-generated code or content, there's a risk of shipping flawed products, writings, or codebases, leading to potential systemic failures and increased societal risk.

---

**Q: How can companies manage the risks associated with deploying AI-generated outputs, and what role does insurance play in this context?**

A: Companies can manage these risks by investing in better verification tooling and cryptographic primitives. Insurance can play a role by putting a dollar value on the risk of AI failures, allowing companies to delegate the responsibility of quantifying and understanding potential issues to specialists. This financial dimension can help manage the complexity and abstraction introduced by AI in the economy.

---

**Q: What is a "verification-grade network effect," and how does it provide a competitive advantage in the age of AI?**

A: A verification-grade network effect is a competitive advantage gained by generating proprietary data that allows for scaling verification from human hands to machines. This data enables better risk underwriting, decision-making, and safer, lower-cost products. Incumbents with vast databases of failure information and startups that prioritize learning from every interaction to improve verification will be more successful.

---

**Q: What are the "hollow economy" and "augmented economy," and what factors distinguish them?**

A: The "hollow economy" is characterized by a decline in training for future verifiers, the codifier's curse, and alignment drift, leading to potential risks and a shrinking class of top verifiers. The "augmented economy," in contrast, leverages AI to accelerate mastery, retrain individuals, and augment human capabilities, leading to a scenario where many things are practically free and new jobs are created.

---

**Q: How can individuals starting their careers best prepare for a future dominated by AI and automation?**

A: Young people should focus on harnessing the power of computers and data to guide machines to accomplish tasks. Instead of focusing on traditional coding, they should learn to control and adapt projects by understanding where attention and resources are needed. The key skill is to have a wide view of an endeavor and constantly adapt it.

---

**Q: What is the role of crypto and blockchain technology in the age of AI, and how are they complementary?**

A: Crypto and blockchain provide deterministic, verifiable infrastructure that complements AI's probabilistic nature. They offer solutions for identity, provenance of digital information, and secure transactions. Blockchain networks can serve as credibly neutral platforms for coordination, information sharing, payments, and insurance, especially as AI leads to more complexity and fragmentation.

---

**Q: How has your research on the economics of AGI influenced your approach to your own work and life?**

A: It has pushed me to rethink every workflow and dare to automate more tasks, even if it feels uncomfortable. I'm also focusing on identifying gaps and side effects created by AI to build solutions that address societal needs. With my kids, I'm focused on accelerated mastery and helping them find their true talents early on.

---

**Q: What are some of the potential negative externalities or risks associated with the increasing automation driven by AI?**

A: One significant risk is the accumulation of technical debt and systemic errors due to the inability to fully verify AI-generated code and content. This can lead to vulnerabilities and failures in critical systems. Additionally, the displacement of human labor and the potential for AI to reinforce existing biases are major concerns that need to be addressed.

---

**Q: What is the significance of open source in the context of AI safety and the potential for abuse?**

A: Open source plays a crucial role in AI safety by allowing for early detection of potential abuses and the development of countermeasures. By deploying open-source models, society can gain early signals of how these systems can be exploited and build defenses accordingly. This is particularly important as proprietary models may have defenses that are easily circumvented.

---

**Q: How are AI models picking up preferences, and what are the implications for their deployment and use in society?**

A: AI models are picking up preferences as side effects of their training, which can be minor or major. These preferences may not be immediately apparent, and as AI is deployed, these preferences can lead to unintended consequences. It's crucial to develop verification infrastructure to act as antibodies against these side effects and ensure AI systems are aligned with human values.

---

**Q: How can society ensure that the benefits of AI are widely distributed and that individuals are empowered to thrive in an AI-driven economy?**

A: Ensuring widespread benefits requires investments in education and retraining programs that focus on developing skills that complement AI, such as critical thinking, creativity, and complex problem-solving. Additionally, policies that support lifelong learning and provide a safety net for those displaced by automation are essential. By empowering individuals to adapt and thrive, society can harness AI's potential while mitigating its risks.
