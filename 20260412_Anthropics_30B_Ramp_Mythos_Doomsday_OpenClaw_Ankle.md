# Summary

The podcast episode centers on the transformative potential and inherent risks of AI, particularly in coding and cybersecurity. The panel debates Anthropic's decision to withhold its Mythos model, assessing whether it's genuine caution or strategic marketing. They explore the competitive landscape between Anthropic and OpenAI, highlighting Anthropic's revenue surge and the possibility of a monopolized coding token market. The conversation then shifts to geopolitical matters, specifically the Iran war, examining the influence of foreign policy on the United States and potential paths toward de-escalation. The group also touches on the impact of AI-driven translation on X, celebrating its ability to foster cross-cultural understanding.

# Q & A

**Q: What is Anthropic doing with its newest AI model, Mythos, and why?**

A: We're withholding Mythos because it's far too dangerous to release widely right now. Our tests showed it autonomously found thousands of vulnerabilities, including bugs in major operating systems and web browsers, some of which were 20-year-old exploits missed by decades of security audits. We're not just finding individual bugs, but also chaining multiple vulnerabilities together to create sophisticated exploits.

---

**Q: How is Anthropic addressing the risks associated with Mythos before releasing it?**

A: We've set up Project Glasswing, an AI-driven cyber coalition with 40 of the most important companies, including Apple, Microsoft, Google, Amazon, and JP Morgan. The goal is to spend 100 days using advanced AI to find, fix, and harden software vulnerabilities before hackers can exploit them. We're talking to the government about this, but we're not relying on top-down regulation.

---

**Q: What is David Sacks' perspective on Anthropic's handling of Mythos and AI safety in general?**

A: Anthropic has a pattern of using fear to market their new products. They roll out a new model and simultaneously release a study showing the worst possible implications of the technology. However, in this specific case with cyber hacking, I think the concern is more legitimate. As coding models become more capable, they're more capable of finding vulnerabilities and stringing them together to create exploits.

---

**Q: What is Chamath Palihapitiya's take on Anthropic's decision to withhold Mythos?**

A: It's mostly theater. In 2019, when Dario was still at OpenAI, they did the same thing with GPT-2, claiming it would unleash a torrent of spam and misinformation. It ended up being a huge nothing burger. If Mythos is truly capable of what they say, a sophisticated hacker can probably do those things right now with Opus. And if these exploits are this easy to find, you'd have to shut down the internet for about five years to patch them all.

---

**Q: What is Brad Gerstner's assessment of Anthropic's recent revenue growth?**

A: Anthropic's revenue explosion is the largest in the history of technology. They added $4 billion of revenue in January, $7 billion in February, and $10 or $11 billion in March. That's Databricks plus Palantir combined in a single month. They were planning to end the year at a $30 billion exit run rate, but they got there by the end of March.

---

**Q: Why is Anthropic experiencing such rapid revenue growth, according to Brad Gerstner?**

A: The model and product capability just hit a threshold near AGI. Companies are realizing this is no longer about IT budgets, but about labor augmentation and labor replacement. We have a near-infinite TAM. The demand for intelligence is radically different than anything we've seen before.

---

**Q: What is Chamath Palihapitiya's perspective on the current state of AI-enabled coding?**

A: AI-enabled coding is still only about 5% of the broad market. Most of the code being written is still white sheet de novo code. The long-horizon ability for any of these models to actually build enterprise-grade software is still sh**. I call on our customers, half a trillion dollar banks, hundred billion dollar insurance companies, and none of these guys are like, wow, it just works out of the box.

---

**Q: What is Jason Calacanis's view on the future of open-source models in the large language model space?**

A: I believe open source is going to win the day on the large language models and take 90% of the token usage. I think the entire frontier model space could be undercut by open source. SLMs, the smaller language models that are verticalized now, will run on desktops and laptops, and that is their biggest competitive threat.

---

**Q: What are David Sacks' thoughts on the potential for AI to monopolize the market for coding tokens?**

A: If you're the early leader in coding as an AI model company, you might get the most training tokens. There is a potential flywheel where you could see the early market leader consolidating its lead because it's generating the most code tokens and it's getting access to the most existing code. So, I do believe that the market for coding tokens could be monopolized.

---

**Q: What is Brad Gerstner's perspective on the competition between OpenAI and Anthropic?**

A: Anthropic was literally counted out of the game last year, and now they've kicked OpenAI's ass over the last 90 days. They made choices: no multimodal, no video, no hardware, no chips, no building data centers. They focused on coding and co-work. But you would be seriously foolish to count out OpenAI. I think we're at peak OpenAI FUD.

**Q: What is Jason Calacanis's assessment of the auto-translate feature on X?**
